Sequences and series

Arithmetic progressions and meansGeometric progressions and meansInfinite geometric sumsSpecial sums and differences
UnderstandExploreQuick checkSolve
01 / Build the idea

The concept, clearly.

A sequence lists ordered terms; a series adds them. Arithmetic progressions have a constant difference, so their graphs of term against index lie on a line. Geometric progressions have a constant ratio. An infinite geometric series has a finite sum only when the absolute ratio is less than one. Special sums of natural numbers, squares and cubes can simplify polynomial series. For positive numbers, AM ≥ GM, with equality when the numbers coincide.

AP: aₙ=a+(n−1)d, Sₙ=n[2a+(n−1)d]/2; GP: aₙ=arⁿ⁻¹, S∞=a/(1−r), |r|<1
Go deeper · methods and formula toolkit

For r≠1, a finite GP sum is a(1−rⁿ)/(1−r); when r=1 it is na. The formula works for negative ratios as well.

Σk=n(n+1)/2; Σk²=n(n+1)(2n+1)/6; Σk³=[n(n+1)/2]², with k from 1 to n. To recover a term from a sum formula, use aₙ=Sₙ−Sₙ₋₁.

For positive a,c, AM=(a+c)/2 and GM=√(ac). To insert m arithmetic means use d=(c−a)/(m+1); for positive endpoints in a GP use r=(c/a)^(1/(m+1)).

Watch for this
An infinite sum with ratio −2 diverges, even though the ratio is less than 1. The condition is |r|<1.

01 · Put it into practice

Find the sum of the first 20 positive integers.

Need a hint?

Use n(n+1)/2.

  1. Use n(n+1)/2.
  2. 20×21/2=210.
Answer: 210
02 · Take it further

Find the infinite sum 8+4+2+… .

Need a hint?

Here a=8 and r=1/2.

Reveal worked solution
  1. Here a=8 and r=1/2.
  2. S∞=8/(1−1/2)=16.
Answer: 16
Worked example

Find the sum 3+6+12+… through 6 terms.

  1. This is a GP with a=3, r=2 and n=6.
  2. Use Sₙ=a(rⁿ−1)/(r−1).
  3. S₆=3(64−1)=189.
